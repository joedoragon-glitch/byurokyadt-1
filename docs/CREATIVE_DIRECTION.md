# Creative direction and authorial principles

**Project:** БЮРОКЯДТ-1 — an original fictional 1982 Soviet document-confirmation apparatus.

This guide adapts the owner's **cross-game** authorial principles to this project's established setting. It is not a mandate to import fantasy-RPG art, combat, story, sound palette or engine architecture. The historical [V9 construction reference](../AESTHETICS.md) has the actual accepted cabinet silhouettes, poster references and texture provenance; consult it before any visual redesign.

## The game's specific identity

The comedy is that a pointless procedure is performed with absolute institutional gravity. The scene feels like a real, inhabited, slightly exhausted bureaucratic office rather than a bright UI joke: enamel machinery, worn metal, paper, heavy furniture, a cold window, a closed corridor door, subdued light and distant human presence. Mechanical operations and official ceremony should look consequential even though the result is absurdly mundane.

Keep the original cabinet recognizable: square inset CRT, switch bank, ministry plate, paired meters, red control, projecting feed bed, press, outlet chute, cabinet hardware and physical counter/tray. Do not flatten it into a generic industrial prop or replace accepted reference art without an owner-directed redesign.

The source's deliberately theatrical pseudo-Cyrillic/Hollywood-Soviet English is part of the satire, **not** a justification for illegible controls. Give accessible labels plain readable language. Real Cyrillic, filenames and text rendering must remain deterministic; do not depend on generative textures to print exact text.

## Reusable authorial principles

- **Specificity over generic spectacle.** Every material, prop, camera view and sound should convey something about the fictional world. More effects do not automatically mean a richer game.
- **Tangible action and consequence.** A pressed button triggers believable mechanisms; a roller moves a sheet; the stamp touches the paper before leaving a mark; the certificate can actually be collected.
- **Coherent atmosphere.** Lighting, environmental motion, sound, scale and pacing work together. Occasional corridor life is stronger than constant random interruption.
- **Playful restraint.** The satire can be quietly absurd without excessive popups, fourth-wall jokes, shouting or compulsory comedy.
- **Respect established artwork.** Work from accepted references, preserve originals and provenance, and distinguish new candidates from approved replacements. Use real scene captures at playable scales for reviews.
- **Player comprehension.** The operator should understand the action, the machine's progress and the outcome. Accessibility and control discoverability are part of the art direction.
- **No cosmetic certification.** A model, sound or texture that loads is not automatically beautiful, readable, believable or accepted. Test in context and state the limits of any unperformed human review.

## Creating or changing assets

Start with the affected in-game shot and current asset/geometry. Record what must remain recognizable, what the proposed edit changes and how it interacts with motion, camera framing, lighting and text. Retain the original reference and replacement candidate separately. Review native desktop and narrow/mobile captures where relevant, including intermediate animation rather than only resting end states.

For soundtrack and mechanical sound, see [Audio direction](AUDIO_DIRECTION.md). For measurable technical/visual gates, see [Quality standards](QUALITY_STANDARDS.md). New features are **proposals** until the owner approves their creative direction; a general request to improve the game does not silently authorize a complete visual or tonal redesign.
